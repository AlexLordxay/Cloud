If you fell into a black hole, you'd see something no living person has ever seen.

This is Sagittarius A-star — the heart of our galaxy. It weighs as much as four million Suns, yet it would fit inside the orbit of Mercury.

In twenty twenty-two, it was photographed for the first time.

What is the event horizon? To escape Earth, you need eleven kilometers per second. Near a black hole, that speed grows — and at the horizon, it equals the speed of light. That's why not even light can get out. It's not a wall — it's a line you never come back from.

A friend far away sees you slow down, turn red — and freeze at the edge. Forever.

But you just keep falling.

Near the edge, light goes around in circles: if you looked back, you could see the back of your own head.

A small black hole would stretch you like spaghetti. But this one is so huge, you'll cross the edge without even noticing.

The sky behind you shrinks into a bright ring.

Less than a minute to the center. And what's there — nobody knows.
