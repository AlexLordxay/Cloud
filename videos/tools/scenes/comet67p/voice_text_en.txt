Two Ukrainians discovered this comet. Forty-five years later, a probe landed on it.

Nineteen sixty-nine. Klim Churyumov and Svitlana Gerasimenko…

…find a new comet on a photographic plate — Comet sixty-seven P.

Two thousand four. The European probe Rosetta sets off to catch it.

Ten years of flight: three times past Earth, and once past Mars.

August twenty fourteen: Rosetta is in orbit. The nucleus looks like a rubber duck.

On November twelfth, Philae becomes the first probe ever to land on a comet.

The harpoons failed — Philae bounced and got stuck in the shadows.

Sixty hours of work, data sent to Earth — and it fell asleep.

Rosetta heard the comet: the ripples in its magnetic field sound like singing.
