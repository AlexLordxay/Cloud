You've seen Halley's Comet. You just didn't know it.

It comes back once every seventy-six years. The last time was in nineteen eighty-six — maybe your parents saw it.

Mark Twain was born the year it appeared. "I came in with Halley's Comet," he said, "and I expect to go out with it."

And he died the day after it returned, in nineteen ten.

Every time the comet passes, it sheds dust. The dust stays on its orbit — a long river of tiny grains.

Every October, Earth flies through that trail. We dive into the tail of Halley's Comet.

Grains smaller than a grain of sand hit the air at sixty-six kilometers per second — sixty times faster than a bullet — and burn up.

These are the Orionids. They peak around October twenty-first. Look after three in the morning, until dawn.

The comet itself returns in twenty sixty-one. How old will you be? Tell me in the comments.
