On Venus, the Sun rises in the west.

It spins the other way from Earth — and so slowly that one turn takes longer than its whole year.

Venus is almost the size of Earth — it's called our planet's twin. In our sky it's the brightest thing after the Moon: people called it the Evening Star and the Morning Star.

But this is true hell: over four hundred and sixty degrees Celsius — hotter than Mercury, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

The pressure is like almost a kilometer deep in the ocean. And the clouds are made of sulfuric acid.

A human here would be crushed, cooked, and dissolved — all at once.

Venus has more than sixteen hundred major volcanoes. This is Maat Mons — it rises almost ten kilometers above the planet's average level.

In nineteen seventy, the Soviet probe Venera 7 sent the first signal ever from the surface of another planet. And Venera 13 sent the first color photos — and survived only about two hours.
