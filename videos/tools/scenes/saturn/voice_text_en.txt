This planet could float in water.

Saturn is the second-largest planet: seven hundred and sixty Earths would fit inside it.

Its rings are as wide as twenty-one Earths, yet only about ten metres thick.

At the north pole spins a six-sided storm. Each of its sides is longer than the Earth is wide.

Hear that?

This is how its radio waves sound, recorded by the Cassini probe.

A day here lasts only ten and a half hours, though Saturn is nine times wider than Earth.

The rings are billions of chunks of ice, from grains of sand to boulders the size of a house.

Titan is bigger than Mercury, and wrapped in a thick atmosphere.

It rains methane there, and there are lakes and seas on its surface.

And a year on Saturn lasts twenty-nine and a half Earth years.
